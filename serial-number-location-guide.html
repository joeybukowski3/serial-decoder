Serial number location guide

Where to Find the Serial Number on Any Appliance or Electronic

A complete guide to locating data plates and model labels — before you call a technician, file a warranty claim, or run a decoder.

Every appliance, HVAC system, and electronic device leaves the factory with a manufacturer data plate or serial label that ties that exact unit to its production history. That small plate can reveal when the item was built, where it was produced, what electrical or fuel specifications it requires, and how it should be identified for service or replacement. The hard part is that manufacturers hide these labels where they are less likely to be damaged by heat, moisture, cleaning, sunlight, or everyday wear. This guide walks through the most common locations by product type and adds brand-specific shortcuts where people most often get stuck.

Determine the age or manufacture date of an item. Verify whether warranty coverage may still apply. Order the correct replacement parts the first time. Document items clearly for an insurance claim. Register the product with the manufacturer.

Major Kitchen Appliances

Kitchen appliances tend to hide serial labels where they stay protected from grease, spills, and repeated cleaning, which usually means just inside a door opening, behind a drawer, or on a frame edge you only notice once the appliance is open.

Refrigerators

On most refrigerators, the first place to check is the upper interior wall of the fresh food compartment. Depending on the brand, that label may be on the left or right side, and it often sits just high enough that it is easy to miss until the door is fully open and the shelves are out of the way. French-door and side-by-side models most often place the label inside the right-hand fresh food section near the top, while top-freezer models frequently use the left interior wall. Bottom-freezer units can be less consistent, so it is worth checking both the main fresh food interior and the area behind the lower crisper drawers where the liner is still visible. If nothing appears inside, inspect the cabinet opening near the door seal and, as a last resort, the rear service panel. Older refrigerators often use a stamped metal plate, while newer models usually rely on a white or silver adhesive label.

Ranges, Ovens & Stoves

Ranges and ovens are one of the few categories where the label is often hidden in plain sight. The most common location is inside the storage drawer at the bottom, either on the drawer frame or along the interior edge of the opening. Electric ranges sometimes move that label to the oven door frame so it becomes visible only when the door is open. Gas ranges occasionally place it on the side of the burner box, accessible from the front once the drawer or lower panel is open. On many double-oven GE and Whirlpool-family models, a flip-up metal tag on the rear of the control panel provides the quickest path and can often be reached without pulling the unit away from the wall. Wall ovens usually keep the plate inside the oven door frame or on the left interior side panel.

Dishwashers

Dishwashers are among the most consistent categories. Open the door fully and inspect both interior edges of the door, especially the side that faces the tub when the door is closed. Some brands shift the label slightly inward onto the tub side wall, so the tag is only visible when the door is open all the way. If it is not on the door edge, look on the left or right interior side of the tub itself. A few older or service-oriented models place the plate behind the kick panel under the door, but that usually requires removing screws.

Microwaves

Countertop microwaves most often place the label on the door frame just inside the opening, though the back panel or one of the side panels is also common. Over-the-range units frequently use the door frame too, but some place the label on the upper interior cavity near the hinge side where it is hidden until the door swings wide. Built-in and drawer microwaves usually keep the plate inside the frame or on the left interior wall of the cavity. If you still cannot find it, the label may be on a rear surface that requires sliding the unit forward slightly.

Freezers

Upright freezers typically place the serial label on the interior side trim where it is visible once the door is open. Chest freezers are more likely to use the exterior left wall above the access panel or the inside rim of the lid. Because cold environments are hard on paper adhesives, many freezers use a metal plate rather than a thin sticker.

Laundry Appliances

Laundry products are built around openings, lids, and removable panels, so the label is usually attached to whichever edge stays protected during use but still remains reachable for service.

Washing Machines (Top Load)

For top-load washers, start under the lid. The label is commonly attached to the rear portion of the lid itself or to the tub rim just inside the lid opening where it stays dry and out of direct abrasion. Some brands, especially Samsung and LG, place the label on the rear of the machine instead, which means the washer may need to be moved away from the wall far enough to photograph the tag safely. If the machine still contains water, do not tip or drag it just to reach the label. It is better to drain it first or use another angle with a phone camera.

Washing Machines (Front Load)

Front-load washers usually place the data label on the fixed door frame rather than on the moving door itself. Look closely at the inner rim of the opening after the door swings open. Some units also place a second label on the rear panel or inside the detergent dispenser area. LG front-loaders commonly use the right-hand side of the door frame, which is easy to miss if you only glance at the hinge side.

Dryers

Both gas and electric dryers usually keep the serial label inside the main door opening, either on the door itself or on the cabinet rim behind it. Another worthwhile check is the lint-trap slot, since some models attach a small label there where owners rarely look. Older dryers may shift the label to the rear panel, and Whirlpool-family units often use the front panel just behind the circular door opening ring.

HVAC Systems

HVAC data plates are usually different from the stickers found on household appliances. Instead of a small product label, you are often dealing with a metal or heavy-duty nameplate that includes far more technical information: electrical ratings, refrigerant type, capacity, and safety listings in addition to the model and serial number. That makes the HVAC plate especially important when you are verifying age, preparing insurance documentation, comparing replacement options, or confirming exactly what type of equipment is installed.

Central Air Conditioner — Outdoor Condenser Unit

On an outdoor condenser, the data plate is usually mounted on an exterior side panel, most often on the same side as the refrigerant line connections. Older units often place it lower on that side panel above the line set, while many newer units move it to an upper corner where it stays cleaner and easier to see. If the exterior plate is missing or damaged, inspect the electrical access panel on the side of the unit because some manufacturers duplicate part of the information there. The plate normally shows the model number, serial number, refrigerant type, electrical specifications, and the unit capacity or tonnage. Because sun and weather can fade outdoor labels badly, it is smart to photograph the plate as soon as you find it.

Furnaces & Indoor Air Handlers

Gas furnaces usually hide the plate behind the front access panel, especially the lower removable door. Once that panel is open, check the interior cabinet wall for a label that stays protected from heat and cleaning. Air handlers, including indoor sections paired with heat pumps, commonly use the inside of the main access panel or the side wall of the cabinet near the blower compartment. High-efficiency furnaces with sealed combustion may also place the label on the exterior side near the flue connection because that side remains visible during service. On older furnaces, the plate may be attached directly to the burner access cover or blower door.

Heat Pumps

Outdoor heat pumps generally use the same label location as a standard air conditioner condenser: the side panel near the refrigerant lines. The difference is in the information on the plate, which should identify the unit as a heat pump rather than a straight-cool air conditioner. The matching indoor air handler usually carries its own label inside the main access panel.

Mini-Split Systems

With mini-splits, open the front filter cover on the indoor wall unit and inspect the interior housing for the label. Some brands instead mount it on the side of the indoor cabinet where it is only visible from an angle. The outdoor mini-split unit uses the familiar side-panel label found on central AC condensers. On multi-zone systems, each indoor head has its own serial number, so do not assume the outdoor unit serial applies to every room unit.

Window & Portable Air Conditioners

Window air conditioners often place the plate behind the front grille or decorative cover, which may need to be removed before the label becomes visible. Others use the back or side exterior panel. Older window units commonly used a durable metal plate behind the front cover, while portable air conditioners usually rely on a rear sticker or a label on the underside of the unit. It is also worth checking the portion of the cabinet that faces the room, since some manufacturers hide the plate there instead of on the outdoor-facing side.

Water Heaters

Tank water heaters, whether gas or electric, usually carry a large label or metal data plate on the upper body of the tank just below the top cap. In many utility rooms, it is designed to face outward so the installer or technician can read it while standing in front of the unit, but some labels wrap around the curve of the tank, so you may need to inspect both the front-facing and side-facing portions to capture the full serial number. Tankless water heaters are usually easier: the label is often on the front face and visible without opening anything, although some brands place a second tag inside the removable front cover. Water heater plates typically include the serial number, BTU rating, flow rate, gas type or electrical requirements, and sometimes an installation-date field handwritten by the installer. Gas tank units may also have a second label down near the burner access area.

Electronics & Devices

Electronics labels are usually smaller than appliance labels and almost always adhesive rather than metal. Many include a QR code or barcode next to the serial number, and modern devices often store the same information in software so you can retrieve it even when the back of the device is inaccessible.

Televisions (Flat Screen — LCD, LED, OLED, QLED)

Most flat-screen TVs place the serial label on the back panel in the lower-right or lower-left area, often close to the power-input section. That sticker usually includes the model number, serial number, and electrical specifications. Samsung, LG, Sony, and other major brands also mirror the serial number in the settings menu under an About, Support, or System Information path. Older plasma sets use the same basic back-panel approach. If the TV is wall-mounted and the rear sticker is difficult to reach, the settings menu is usually the fastest solution.

Laptops & Notebook Computers

On most laptops, the first place to look is the bottom case. Older removable-battery models sometimes hide part or all of the serial label inside the battery compartment, so removing the battery can reveal what the underside sticker does not show. Microsoft Surface devices, MacBooks, and many ultrabooks may have the serial engraved or printed directly onto the bottom case instead of using a paper sticker. The same number is often available through system information on both Windows and macOS. Some HP and Dell models also place an identifier inside the battery bay.

Desktop Computers

Tower and mid-tower desktops usually place the serial number on the back panel, a side panel, or the underside of the case. All-in-one computers often move the label to the rear of the display housing instead. Windows systems can also expose the serial in system settings or system information tools, which is helpful when the case is tucked into a desk enclosure. Servers and workstations often attach the label near the power-supply area or top rear of the chassis.

Smartphones & Tablets

On iPhones, the most reliable path is Settings, then General, then About, where the serial number appears alongside other device identifiers. Some models also print it on the SIM tray, and older pre-iPhone X units sometimes show it on the back near the bottom. Android devices usually place it under About Phone, while older removable-battery phones may still keep a sticker underneath the battery. iPads often print the serial on the back near the lower edge and also show it in Settings. Samsung tablets typically offer both a rear label and a software About screen. Keep in mind that the IMEI is not the same as the serial number.

Gaming Consoles

PlayStation 5 units place the label on the back near the power connection, while PlayStation 4 systems usually keep it on the underside. Xbox Series X and Series S consoles both use rear labels near the port area. Nintendo Switch devices carry the serial on the back of the handheld unit, and the dock has its own separate serial. Most major consoles also display the serial number in their settings menus, which is useful when the hardware is mounted or cabled tightly.

Printers

Printers often put the label on the back panel or the underside, but inkjet models sometimes hide it inside the cartridge-access area where the user would not normally look. Laser printers tend to keep the plate near the rear power connection. Many HP printers also expose the serial number through the touchscreen menu under a printer-information or tools path.

Brand-Specific Notes

Brands often follow their own label habits, and once you know those habits you can usually shorten the search dramatically.

Whirlpool / Maytag / KitchenAid / Amana

These Whirlpool-family brands usually share the same label structure and often use a white sticker with Whirlpool branding at the top. The serial number typically appears directly below the model number, which makes the plate easy to recognize once you have found it. For laundry products, the label is usually inside the door opening or under the lid. Refrigerators often keep it on the upper interior left wall, while ranges frequently use the storage drawer frame.

GE / Hotpoint / GE Profile

GE-family labels are usually white and clearly branded, and the serial number often begins with two letters that carry date significance. In many GE serials, the first letter represents the month and the second letter represents the year in a repeating 12-year cycle. Refrigerators commonly use the right interior wall, ranges use either the door frame or storage drawer area, and dishwashers usually place the label on the inside left door edge.

Samsung

Samsung is less consistent than many older appliance brands, and its labels often include a QR code beside the model and serial fields. Many Samsung washers and some refrigerators place the main label on the rear of the unit, which surprises owners who expect it inside the opening. On electronics, Samsung usually uses a rear-panel sticker, and TVs also expose the same information through the support or about menu.

LG

LG labels are usually cleanly formatted and often place the most important identifiers close together. On appliances, front-load washers and dryers commonly use the right-side portion of the door frame, while refrigerators often use the right interior wall. Ranges may place the tag on the left door frame or around the drawer opening. LG televisions keep the label on the back panel and usually mirror it in the on-screen settings menu.

Carrier / Bryant

Carrier and Bryant HVAC equipment typically use metal data plates rather than paper stickers, and those plates are meant to survive outdoor exposure. The serial number commonly encodes the week and year of manufacture in digits three through six. On condensers, start on the side panel. On furnaces, open the access door and inspect the interior cabinet wall.

Goodman / Amana HVAC

Goodman outdoor units and furnaces usually rely on a metal data plate attached to a side panel or cabinet interior. The serial number often begins with two digits for the year and two digits for the week of manufacture, which makes it especially useful once the plate is found. Outdoor units usually show it plainly on the side panel, while furnaces keep it inside the cabinet.

Trane / American Standard

Trane-family outdoor units usually place the plate on the side panel near the refrigerant lines, and indoor units keep it inside the main access panel. The serial number often begins with a factory letter followed by a two-digit year and a two-digit week. Because the format can be hard to read on weathered units, photographing the plate is usually the safest approach.

Rheem / Ruud

Rheem and Ruud water heaters typically place the main label on the front of the upper tank body, while their HVAC products follow the usual outdoor side-panel and furnace-access-panel pattern. Their serial numbers commonly encode the week first and then the year, which is one reason the exact label matters so much. If the front-facing water-heater label wraps around the tank, inspect the side too.

Apple

Apple devices are among the easiest to verify in software because nearly every product exposes the serial number through system settings. Mac laptops and iPads often also have the serial engraved or printed on the bottom case, while iPhones may use the SIM tray or a small exterior print depending on the generation. Older Apple hardware can still hide identifiers inside battery compartments, but software is usually the fastest path.

Tips for Hard-to-Read Labels

When a label is hard to read, the instinct is usually to lean closer and squint, but a better approach is to improve the lighting and capture the label indirectly. A flashlight held from the side will often make faint printed characters stand out more clearly than direct overhead light. In tight spaces, it is usually easier to photograph the label with your phone and zoom in afterward than to read it while crouched behind a machine. Wipe dust from the plate with a dry cloth before trying to read it, but avoid water or cleaning sprays because moisture can damage old paper labels or blur already-fading ink. Outdoor HVAC units sometimes hide the plate behind a clear plastic protective sleeve, so inspect the edges of the panel before assuming the label is missing. FAQ

Where is the serial number on a refrigerator?

On most refrigerators the serial number label is inside the fresh food compartment. Check the upper-left or upper-right interior wall first, then look behind the crisper drawers or on the door frame if the obvious spot is empty.

Where is the serial number on a washing machine?

Front-load washers usually place it on the door frame or fixed inner rim. Top-load units usually keep it under the lid on the tub rim or lid frame, while some Samsung models place it on the rear of the cabinet.

Where is the serial number on an HVAC unit?

Outdoor condensers usually use a side-panel data plate near the refrigerant lines or electrical disconnect. Furnaces and air handlers usually place the label inside the access panel or on an interior cabinet wall.

Where is the serial number on a TV?

Flat-screen TVs almost always use a sticker on the back panel, often near the lower corners. If the set is wall-mounted, the serial number is usually also available through the settings menu under About or System Information.

Why do I need to find my serial number?

Serial numbers help determine manufacture date and age, verify warranty status, order the right parts, document property for claims, and register the product with the manufacturer.
