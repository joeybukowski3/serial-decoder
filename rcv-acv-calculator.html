What is RCV?

Replacement Cost Value (RCV) is what it would cost to buy a new, comparable item today — before any depreciation is applied. It's the starting point for a depreciation calculation, not the amount typically paid out for an aged item.

payments

What is ACV?

Actual Cash Value (ACV) is the replacement cost minus depreciation for age, wear, and condition. It's a commonly referenced figure in insurance and asset-valuation contexts, though exact methodology varies by who is calculating it.

timeline

How depreciation works conceptually

This calculator multiplies a published annual insurance depreciation rate by item age: Annual Rate × Age = Calculated Depreciation. That result is then adjusted by any optional manual adjustment and capped at the Maximum Total Depreciation you select, so an old item never depreciates past whatever limit you've set.

Two things determine that estimate: how old the item actually is, and which annual rate applies to that item type. Getting the age right matters as much as picking the right rate, because both feed directly into the same multiplication.

event

Why item age matters

Age is multiplied directly against the annual rate — small errors compound. An item mistakenly assumed to be 3 years older than it actually is can noticeably understate its value. This is exactly the kind of gap that serial number decoding and Smart Lookup are built to close: confirming manufacture date from the label itself rather than guessing.

hourglass_top

Why the annual rate matters

The Item Type selector loads a published annual depreciation rate from the Claims Pages insurance depreciation reference for items it covers. Different item types depreciate at very different annual rates, and items without a verified rate require you to enter one directly — this calculator never guesses a rate on your behalf.

balance

Why two people may reach different depreciation conclusions

Different rate references Common source of variance

Insurers, adjusters, and independent researchers don't all reference the same annual depreciation rate for the same product category.

Different maximum caps Policy-dependent

A policy or methodology might cap total depreciation at 75%, 90%, or another figure entirely — there's no single universal ceiling.

Condition adjustments Judgment call

Visible wear, maintenance history, and functional condition can shift a depreciation figure beyond pure age math — and reasonable people can disagree on by how much.

Jurisdiction & policy language Legal/contractual

Depreciation methodology can be shaped by state regulation and the specific language of a policy or contract, which this calculator does not interpret.

Why this is an estimate, not a settlement determination

This calculator applies one specific, transparent methodology: a published Claims Pages annual depreciation rate multiplied by age, with a user-set maximum. It does not know your policy language, jurisdiction, insurer's internal methodology, or the item's physical condition. Real depreciation determinations can and do use different rate references, different caps, and different condition adjustments. Use this tool to understand the math and produce a documented starting estimate — not as a final coverage or payout figure.

FAQ

Where do the annual depreciation rates come from? expand_more

From the current, live Claims Pages depreciation calculator, a commonly referenced insurance-industry depreciation source. Each item's rate and source link are shown when you select it.

Is 75% the industry-standard maximum total depreciation? expand_more

No. 75% is simply this calculator's default starting value, meaning at least 25% of replacement value always remains. Maximum depreciation caps vary by insurer, policy, item type, and jurisdiction — change the dropdown to whatever maximum applies to your situation, or choose Custom.

Does this calculator determine my insurance settlement? expand_more

No. It applies one depreciation methodology (published annual rate × age, with your chosen cap) to produce an estimate. Actual settlements depend on policy language, your insurer's methodology, jurisdiction, and item condition.

What does the manual adjustment field do? expand_more

It lets you shift the calculated depreciation up or down by a percentage-point amount you choose, for a reason you supply — for example, documented condition. It's applied before the maximum total depreciation cap, never applies automatically, and stays visible in the calculation details rather than silently changing the result.

What if my item type isn't in the list, or has no verified rate? expand_more

Choose the closest "Not Yet Verified" entry or Other / Custom, then enter the annual depreciation rate required by your claim or estimating methodology. The calculator never guesses a rate for items without a verified source.

How do I get an accurate item age to use here? expand_more

Use the serial number decoder or Smart Lookup to research the manufacture date from the item's label, then use that confirmed age here.

Can I use decimal values for age? expand_more

Yes. The age field accepts decimal years (for example, 4.5 years) for more precise results.
